The route so far

Not a straight academic track. A transfer from a marketing diploma into the degree years.

Origin

Higher Diploma in Marketing, HKU SPACE

I completed a Higher Diploma in Marketing at HKU SPACE before moving on. That programme gave me the marketing base I am still using: how a message is built, who it is for, and how a campaign has to work outside the classroom.

Interchange

Into Year 3 at Hang Seng University

After graduating from HKU SPACE, I continued into the BBA-Marketing programme at The Hang Seng University of Hong Kong, starting from Year 3 rather than Year 1. The change of campus also meant a change of pace: more degree-level analysis on top of the diploma training.

Current stop

Year 4, BBA-Marketing

I am now a Year 4 student. This year is about closing the degree and getting clearer on the kind of marketing work I actually want to do, which for me is events rather than only screen-based campaigns.

What marketing means here

The site is about the path, not a project gallery.

Why the diploma still matters

HKU SPACE was not a detour. It is the reason I could enter the degree at Year 3. I treat that as part of the story: applied marketing first, then a fuller BBA at HSUHK. Visitors should be able to see both institutions without guessing which one I am in now.

What I am aiming at

Event marketing sits between a brand and a live crowd. Timing, place, partnerships and the atmosphere of the day all matter. Watching major sport has been an informal lesson in that: a match or a Games is already an event people plan their week around.

What I watch

Tap a card. I follow the event, not only the score.

Cycling Stage races and one-day races. The route itself is the product.

I like how a race is staged across cities and weather, and how a sponsor stays visible for hours without a hard sell. That is useful to think about for event branding.

Basketball Club nights and international windows. Fast crowd, short attention.

Basketball shows a different event rhythm: timeouts, entertainment breaks, and a crowd that reacts in seconds. Good practice for noticing what keeps people in the room.

Football League weekends and tournament nights.

Football is the sport I come back to most. A derby or a final is marketing and community at the same time: shirts, songs, travel, and a kick-off that structures the whole day.

Olympics Asian Games World Cup

The big multi-sport calendar

I also follow the Olympics, the Asian Games and the World Cup. They are the clearest examples I know of an event brand that has to work across languages, host cities and weeks of coverage. That is the scale I find interesting, even if a first job will be much smaller.

Next stop

Career
Event marketer

I would like to work as an event marketer: planning how people hear about an event, why they should turn up, and how the brand feels on the ground. Study gave me the marketing language. Sport gave me a habit of watching how live occasions are packaged.
